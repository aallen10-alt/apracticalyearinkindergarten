Phonics — Letter b — /b/ sound · bat-big-bus-bed-bug · slides only
Alphablocks →
SAY: "Hold up the letter B card. 'This is the letter B. It makes the /b/ sound. Put both lips together and then let them pop apart — /b/. Feel that quick pop? /b/. Not buh — just /b/. It is a fast sound. Say it: /b/. /b/. /b/.' Point to picture. 'The picture is a bat. Bat starts with B. First sound in BAT? /b/.'"
Today's words — tap sounds, then read:
BATBIGBUSBEDBUGBITBOBBUN
SAY: "Hold up BAT. 'Tap the sounds with me — /b/ [tap] /ă/ [tap] /t/ [tap]. Three sounds. Read it — BAT.' Hold up BIG. 'Your turn — tap before you read. Go.' Students tap /b/ /ĭ/ /g/ then read BIG. Hold up BUS. Students tap and read independently. Then show word cards mixed with known letters — students sort: does this start with B or not? CAT — not B. BED — B. NAP — not B. CUP — not B. BUG — B."
🧰 WAIT TIME: Ask the question. Count silently to 10. Then call on someone. 10 seconds. Every time.
⚡
Snap Word — "to" — T — O — introduce and add to the wall.
SAY: "Hold up TO. 'Snap word — TO. T-O. Just two letters. Spell it: T, O. I am going TO school. I want TO read. Write it in the air: T, O.' Add to wall. 'Seven snap words! I, SEE, A, THE, AND, WE, TO. Read them from the wall.'"

Math · Unit 1 Lesson 15 · Compare groups — which has more, fewer, same? · numbers to 10
Hook → mini lesson → seat work → 2 rotations × 20 min
~75 min
K.CC.C.6 K.CC.C.7
Objective
Compare groups — which has more, fewer, same? · numbers to 10
🔵
Hook on bumblebee spots — 3 min — spark curiosity — do NOT teach yet.
SAY: "Put two groups on the rug — 6 cubes and 9 cubes. 'Two groups. Before counting — just look. Which do you think has more? Which has fewer? Take a guess before you count at smartboard spots.'"
🎵 SING: "Smartboard spots, smartboard spots, find your smartboard spot!"
📐
Mini lesson — smartboard spots — 8 minutes MAX — word for word.
SAY: "'We are comparing two groups. More means the bigger amount. Fewer means the smaller amount. Same means they are equal — the same number.' Count both groups together: '6 cubes. 9 cubes. Which has more — 6 or 9?' Students answer. '9 has MORE. 6 has FEWER. Write on your paper: the [blue] group has more. The [red] group has fewer.' Do 3 more comparisons with groups up to 10. Students draw two groups on recording sheet, count each, and circle the group that has more."
🧰 8 MINUTE RULE: Hard cap. Everything else happens at small group table. Extended whole group = more behavior problems.

Writing · Unit 1 · Drawing to tell a story — wrap up · what makes a story complete?
Mini lesson 5 min → independent 15 min
~20 min
W.K.2W.K.3L.K.1L.K.2
Objective
Drawing to tell a story — wrap up · what makes a story complete?
🖊️
Model on chart paper first. Then students write in folders.
SAY: "'We have been drawing stories all month. Before we start our next unit, I want to look at what makes a great story drawing.' [Display 3 student examples — with permission] 'This one has a character, a setting, and something happening — that is a complete story. This one has details and labels — I know exactly what is going on. What do you notice that makes these great?' Students share observations. 'Today — make sure your story has a character doing something. Not just a drawing of a thing — a story. Something happens. Draw that.'"
Conference with 3 students minimum. One question: "Tell me about your writing." One suggestion. Move on.
