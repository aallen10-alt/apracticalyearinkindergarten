Whole Group Lesson — Counting Objects to 10
15 min
NJ: K.CC.B.4 — Understanding number-quantity relationship
📋 Anchor Chart — Build With Students Today
HOW TO COUNT
  • Touch each object ONE time
  • Say one number for each touch
  • The LAST number you say = HOW MANY
Build this chart as you teach — don't pre-make it. Draw as you go. Their ideas go on the chart. It's more powerful when they watch it being created.
SAY: "I have some cubes. Watch my finger. One — [touch] — two — [touch] — three — [touch]. How many cubes do I have? Three. The last number I said is the answer. The last number WINS. Now try — hold up 5 fingers. Count with me. Touch each finger. One, two, three, four, five. How many fingers? Five — the last number."
🛒 Linking Cubes — Amazon 🛒 Counting Bears — Amazon 🛒 Ten Frame Counters — Amazon
💡 STRATEGY — Can't count past 3 with one-to-one correspondence: Put a sticker on each object they're counting. They peel the sticker as they count it. The physical peeling slows them down and forces one-to-one matching. No stickers? They turn objects over as they count. Counted = face down. Uncounted = face up.
💡 STRATEGY — Skips numbers or loses track: Use a number line on their desk. As they count each object, they point to the next number on the number line. Two anchors at once — the object AND the number line — keep them on track.
4
Math Centers — 2 rotations, 15 min each
35 min · Small group light on 🔴
Center A — Counting Mats

Place counting bears or cubes on numbered mats 1-10. Count the objects. Write the number. When done — draw their own set and write the number.

Center B — Math Shelf

Math shelf exploration. Pattern blocks, linking cubes, dominoes. One item at a time. Return before taking another. Quiet and independent.

🔴 Small Group Pull: While centers run, pull your 3 lowest math students. Give each student 5 objects. "Count these for me." Watch their strategy. Do they use one-to-one? Do they skip numbers? This assessment takes 3 minutes per student and tells you exactly what they need.
💡 STRATEGY — Meltdowns at center transitions: Give a "first-then" card. "First counting mat, then you pick one thing from the math shelf." The visual removes the argument. Sequence provides certainty. Certainty reduces anxiety.

🧰 Teacher Toolbox — Strategies for Every Type of Learner Today ▼
🧠 Students Who Can't Sit Still — Specific to Today's Lessons ▼

The real issue: Five-year-old attention maxes out at 5-8 minutes per task. You're asking them to sit for 35 minutes of structured literacy. Their bodies are not broken — the task length is the challenge.

During whiteboard work specifically: Let students who cannot stay seated stand at their chairs. Standing is not a behavior problem — it is a regulation strategy. Their writing will actually improve because their core is engaged.

During letter sound card review: Let the most wiggly student be the card holder. They stand next to you and hold each card up for the class. They are moving, they have a job, they are learning.

During Alphablocks™: Give them a physical response for each sound — karate chop for K, wave for W, victory sign for V. Movement anchors attention in young children.

  • Brain break every 8 minutes — non-negotiable in September
  • Stand-at-chair option during any seat work
  • Fidget tool at seat — stress ball, resistance band on chair leg
  • Seat them within arm's reach during whole group
  • Pre-warn before every transition: "Two more minutes, then we go to the rug"
📚 Struggling Readers — Tier 1 and Tier 2 Strategies for Today ▼

What research says: Students who don't have letter sounds by end of kindergarten are at significant risk for reading difficulties. Intervene early. The window is September through December.

During today's lesson specifically:

  • Errorless learning — whisper the sound in their ear a half-second before the group responds. They say it with the group. They feel successful. Fade the prompt as they gain independence.
  • Multi-sensory — say the letter, trace it in the air, write on whiteboard, tap it on the desk. Four modalities per letter. Research shows multi-sensory instruction produces significantly better retention.
  • Reduce the set — if a student knows 5 of 15 letters, drill only those 5 on their personal cards during morning pull. Don't add new letters until current ones are automatic.

Tier 2 signal: If a student cannot identify any letter sounds by Week 4, start I&RS documentation. Do not wait until November. Eight weeks of documented intervention is required before referral. Week 4 is your last safe starting point.

➕ Struggling with Counting and One-to-One Correspondence ▼

What today's struggle looks like: They count an object twice, skip one, or say "five" while touching the third object. Normal in September. Not normal by December.

Strategies specific to today's counting lesson:

  • Turn objects over as they count — uncounted face up, counted face down. Physical movement forces one-to-one.
  • Line objects up before counting — scattered objects are harder to track than objects in a row. Teach them: organize first, count second.
  • Stickers on each object — they peel as they count. Peeling slows them down. Slowness is the strategy.
  • Number line anchor — point to the number line as they count each object. Two anchors keep them on track.

Do not rush to 10: A student who cannot count to 5 reliably is not ready for 10. Accuracy before quantity.

✏️ Struggling Writers — Pencil Grip, Fine Motor, Getting Ideas Down ▼

Pencil grip today: September Week 2 is the time to establish correct grip. Not to battle about it — to teach it and return to it daily for the first 6 weeks.

  • Tissue paper trick — put a small piece of tissue in their palm. They hold it while writing. Cannot make a fist. Fingers naturally relax.
  • Triangular crayons — wider diameter, easier for developing hands. Keep a supply in your desk.
  • Golf pencils — shorter = naturally better grip. Less pencil to hold.

For students who say "I can't draw a person": The stages of drawing development in kindergarten are normal. A circle with lines coming out is developmentally appropriate. Celebrate what is there, not what is missing.

Scribe for students who completely shut down: "Tell me about your picture. I'll write your words and then you can trace them." You are modeling what writing looks like. They are not failing.

Refer to OT if by Week 4: A student cannot hold any writing tool, hand fatigues in under 60 seconds, or shows pain when writing.

🌡️ Behavior and Dysregulation — What to Do Right Now ▼

When a student is melting down during structured literacy: Do not stop the lesson for the whole class. Make eye contact with the student. Lower your voice — do not raise it. Walk over slowly. Get at their level. Say quietly: "I see you. I'm going to keep teaching for two minutes and then I'm coming to you." Then come to them. Your calm is the intervention.

Big problem or small problem framework:

  • Small problem — someone looked at my whiteboard, my marker dried out, I dropped something. You handle it yourself.
  • Big problem — someone is hurt, something won't stop after you asked. You come get me.

During SuccessMaker™ specifically: Technology frustration is a real trigger for kids with low frustration tolerance. If a student slams the laptop or pushes it away — move them away from the computer quietly. "You're going to take a break from the computer. Come sit next to me." No lecture in the moment. Address it privately after others are settled.

The small group light system 🔴: When it's on — you do not come to the small group table unless you are bleeding or someone is in danger. Any other question — hand on head, wait. The other adult in the room addresses it. You do not break small group.

📋 IEP, 504 & RTI — Accommodations for Today's Specific Lessons ▼

During SuccessMaker™: Students with processing speed accommodations may need extended time to log in and navigate. Pair them with a patient peer who will wait. Not the fastest student in the class.

During letter sound cards: Students with phonological processing difficulties need more repetition, not faster presentation. Slowing down the card pace for whole-group review helps every student — not just the ones with IEPs.

During whiteboard work: Students with fine motor IEP goals may use a magnetic letter board instead of writing letters. Same skill, different tool. The manipulation of physical letters meets the same phonics objective.

During math: Students with counting and cardinality IEP goals should have a personal number line taped to their desk all year. Non-negotiable. It is not a crutch — it is an accommodation.

During writing: Students with written expression goals — scribe is always an option. Write their words exactly as they say them. Have them trace or copy one sentence. Document what you did.

RTI documentation note: If you're pulling the same 3 students every morning and they're not progressing by Week 4 — start I&RS paperwork. Week 4 is your last safe starting point to meet the 8-week documentation requirement before holiday break.

🫁 Nervous System Regulation — Quick Tools for Today ▼

Before whiteboard work: 30-second box breathing. In for 4, hold for 4, out for 4, hold for 4. Repeat twice. A regulated nervous system writes better than a dysregulated one.

After math center transition: Brain shake — shake hands loose, shake arms, shake shoulders, shake whole body, freeze. 30 seconds. Releases transition tension before writing.

Before specials: Starfish breathing — trace up each finger breathing in, down breathing out. Five fingers, five breaths. Lands their nervous system before transitioning to a new adult and environment.

For the student who comes in already dysregulated: Do not engage with the behavior. Put a piece of paper and a crayon on their desk. Say nothing. They will draw. Drawing is regulating. When they are calm — and only then — address what happened.

Mindful Marshmallow 🍬 — NJSLS-SEL 2.1.2.A.1 / CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.SL.K.1 — Can be used any time you feel the room is too activated. Hold it. Look at it. Smell it. Take the tiniest bite possible. Chew slowly. Debrief: "When we slow down and pay attention to one thing, our brain gets quiet." ⭐ Always check allergens. Substitute a smooth stone or cotton ball if needed.
