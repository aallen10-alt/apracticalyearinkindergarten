Sheep timer → bumblebee spots → Wednesday greeting → PA Activity 1 → PA Activity 2 → sing to smartboard spots → morning message → letter sound cards → phonics → brain break → math.
⭐ WEDNESDAY — ALPHABLOCKS + PACKET: Alphablocks plays on smartboard. Another adult distributes phonics packets during the episode. Students complete packet at seats after watching. Timer is visible.
⭐ ORDER — ALWAYS: Sheep timer → bumblebee spots → greeting → PA 1 → PA 2 → sing to smartboard → morning message → letter sound cards → phonics → brain break → math. Same every single day. Predictability is the behavior plan.

Phonics Lesson — Letter p — /p/ sound · pat-pit-pop-pan-pet · Alphablocks + packet
Alphablocks →
SAY: "Hold up the letter P card. 'This is the letter P. It makes the /p/ sound. Put your lips together and pop them apart — /p/. Feel that little puff of air? That is the P sound. /p/. Not puh — just /p/. Say it with me — /p/. /p/. /p/.' Point to picture. 'The picture is a pig. Pig starts with /p/. What's the first sound? /p/.'"
Today's words — say each one, tap the sounds, read together:
PATPITPOPPANPETPUPPINPOT
SAY: "'Today we watch Alphablocks first — then we do our packet.' Play the Alphablocks P episode. Students watch — no talking. After the video: 'What sound did we just learn? /p/. Good. Now your packet is coming to you.' Other adult distributes packets during the video. 'Open your packet to page 1. Look at the pictures. Circle every picture that starts with /p/. Say the name of each picture first before you circle it. First picture — say the name. Does it start with /p/? Then circle it.' Circulate. Check that students are saying the words aloud not just guessing."
🧰 WAIT TIME: After you ask a question — count silently to 10 before calling on anyone. 10 seconds feels long. It is the difference between 3 kids answering and 22 kids thinking.
⚡
Snap Word — "a" — introduce and add to the snap word wall.
SAY: "Snap fingers. 'Snap word! This one is so short — just one letter. The word is A. Just A. Spell it with me — A. One letter. Write it on your paper. Write it again. We use this word all the time: I see A dog. I have A pencil. It means one of something.' Add to wall. Count: 'Three snap words — I, SEE, A.'"
Snap words so far: I, see, a — count the words on the wall together.

Math · Unit 1 Lesson 7 · Count to 5 — write numerals 1-5 · number formation practice
Hook → mini lesson → seat work → 2 rotations
~75 min
K.CC.A.3
Objective
Count to 5 — write numerals 1-5 · number formation practice
🔵
Hook on bumblebee spots — 3 minutes — spark curiosity — do NOT teach yet.
SAY: "Hold up a card with 4 dots. 'How many dots? Count them. Now — can you write the number that tells how many? Think about how to write a 4 before we go to smartboard spots.'"
🎵 SING: "Smartboard spots, smartboard spots, find your smartboard spot!"
📐
Mini lesson — smartboard spots — 8 minutes MAX — manipulatives — word for word script.
SAY: "'We have been counting objects. Today we write the number that tells how many. Writing numbers is just like writing letters — there is a correct way and a backwards way.' Show on board: 'The number 1 — start at the top, straight line down. 1.' Students write in air. '2 — start at the top, curve around, slide to the right. 2.' Air write. '3 — curve in, curve in again. 3.' Air write. '4 — down, across, down past the crossing line. 4.' Air write. '5 — down, curve around, put a hat on it. 5.' Air write. Students practice writing 1-5 on their recording sheet. Count dots on a card, write the numeral."
🧰 8 MINUTE RULE: Hard cap on whole group instruction. Everything else happens at the small group table. Extending whole group = more off-task behavior, not more learning.

Writing · Unit 1 · Drawing to tell a story — add speech bubbles · what did the people in your story say?
Mini lesson 5 min → independent practice 15 min → share
~20 min
W.K.3SL.K.4SL.K.5
Objective
Drawing to tell a story — add speech bubbles · what did the people in your story say?
🖊️
Model on chart paper first — then students write independently.
SAY: "'Real authors put words in their characters' mouths. We call this a speech bubble.' [Draw a speech bubble coming from a character's mouth] 'The bubble shows what the person is saying. Watch me — my character is saying HI. I write HI in the bubble.' [Write it] 'You do not need to spell it perfectly. Write the sounds you hear. If your character is saying HELP — what do you hear first? /h/ — write that. What next? /ĕ/ — write that. What last? /l/ /p/ — write those. HELP. Now go add speech bubbles to your story drawings.'"
Conference with 3 students minimum. One question only: "Tell me about your drawing." One suggestion. Move on.
