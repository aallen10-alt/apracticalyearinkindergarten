Morning Arrival Routine
The first 20 minutes set the tone for the entire day
Before Students Arrive — Non-Negotiable
Morning work is already on the tables before the first student walks in. The sheep timer is already set and counting down. The smartboard is already on. The schedule is already posted. Students walk into a room that is ready for them — this signals safety and predictability from the first second.
Morning Work by Day — Same Every Week
Monday → Journal at seats
Tuesday → Computers · SuccessMaker
Wednesday → ELA practice page at seats
Thursday → Math practice page at seats
Friday → Free choice at seats
🧰 TIP: Students who always know what to do when they arrive are students who don't need you the moment they walk in. This buys time to greet every student at the door individually — which is the most important thing that happens all morning.
🐑 The Sheep Timer — Beat It Every Morning
The sheep timer is set before students arrive. It is counting down when they walk in. The expectation: when the sheep timer goes off, students are already in their bumblebee spots — not racing there, already there. This takes two weeks to become automatic. Reinforce it every single day until it is.
SAY: "The challenge every morning is to beat the sheep. If the sheep goes off and you're already on your spot — you win. Can you beat it today?"
🧰 TIP: The timer is not a punishment. It is a game. Kids who love to win will be on their spots before it goes off. Frame it that way always.

Morning Meeting Sequence — Locked Order
Same every day · Students know what comes next · That is the behavior management
  1. Students walk in → go to morning work → begin immediately
  2. Sheep timer goes off → students already at bumblebee spots
  3. Greeting on bumblebee spots
  4. Activity 1 on bumblebee spots — phonological awareness
  5. Activity 2 on bumblebee spots — phonological awareness
  6. Sing to smartboard spots
  7. Read morning message together at smartboard spots
  8. Letter sound cards at smartboard spots — 3 minutes — every day — non-negotiable
  9. Brain break — 2 minutes
  10. Into structured literacy
Bumblebee Spots vs Smartboard Spots
Bumblebee spots = assigned rug spots. Every student has one. Name is on it. No negotiation.

Smartboard spots = spots in front of the smartboard. Students move there to see the board.
🎵 TRANSITION SONG: "Smartboard spots, smartboard spots, find your smartboard spot!" Clap and point. Students move. The song is the signal — not your voice.
🧰 TIP: Songs do transitions. Your voice does not. Once a song becomes the signal, you stop having to ask anyone to move.

Whiteboard Routine — Tuesdays and Thursdays
Never hand out whiteboards yourself · This is a student job
Helper of the Day Distributes Whiteboards
Helper of the day passes whiteboards to every student. Helper picks one friend to pass the markers.
SAY: "[Name], you are our helper today. Please pass one whiteboard to every friend. Pick someone to help you pass the markers."
Once everyone has a whiteboard and marker:
SAY: "Pick up your marker. Take the cap off — put the cap on the back end so it doesn't get cold. Show me your marker is ready."
After every whiteboard activity:
SAY: "Cap your marker — put the hat back on so it doesn't get cold. Put your whiteboard face down on the floor in front of you."
🧰 TIP: Markers that dry out cost money. Teaching students to cap their markers is not a nicety — it is an economics lesson.

Math Routine — Every Day
Hook → Teach → Seat work → Groups → Numberblocks · Must finish before paraprofessional lunch
  1. Hook on bumblebee spots (3 min) — Hold up a manipulative. Ask one curiosity question. Do not teach yet.
  2. Teach on smartboard spots (8 min MAX) — Use manipulatives. Original script — no alien videos. Students handle their own manipulatives at their spots.
  3. Brain break (2 min) — Reset bodies before seat work.
  4. Seat work with visible timer (8 min) — Students work independently. Timer is visible. When it goes off — pencils down. Not finished = accountability slip home that night.
  5. Numberblocks video (3 min) — Plays during group transition. Previews tomorrow's concept.
  6. 2 rotations × 20 min — Teacher table, Adult 2 table, math shelf, SuccessMaker.
⏰ The Timer and the Accountability Slip
When the seat work timer goes off and a student has not finished — quiet conversation. Not public. "The timer went off and you didn't finish. What happened?" Then fill out the accountability slip together. Student circles: was I focused? Did I ask for help? Did I finish? Draw a picture. Goes home for parent signature.
🧰 TIP: Do this once consistently and it almost never needs to happen again. Students learn that the timer is real.
🗣️ How to Ask for Help — Taught Explicitly Week 1
Not acceptable: "I don't get it." "I don't know." "I don't understand."

Required: "I don't understand how to ____." "I need help with ____."
SAY: "If you need help, I need you to tell me specifically what you need help with. 'I don't get it' doesn't help me help you. Try again — 'I don't understand how to ___.' Say that with me."
Reinforce this every time a student says "I don't get it" — redirect them to the correct language before helping.
⚠️ Math Finishes Before Paraprofessional Lunch
Math must be complete before approximately 11:45 when the paraprofessional goes to lunch. If the lesson is running long — cut seat work and move it to the teacher table during group time. Do not let math bleed into writing or shared reading time. Non-negotiable.

Lunch Dismissal — By Ticket Color
Same order every day · Teach it Week 1 · Practice it until automatic
Ticket Color Dismissal Order
SAY: "Red tickets — stand up, push in your chair, line up at the door. Blue tickets — stand up, push in your chair, line up. White tickets — stand up, push in your chair, line up. Lunchboxes — stand up, push in your chair, line up."
Hot lava line to the cafeteria. Quiet in the hallway. Personal space. Same every day.
🧰 TIP: Practice dismissal order during the first week until students can tell you their ticket color without looking. This prevents the lunchtime pile-up at the door every day for the rest of the year.

Teacher's Read Aloud — After Lunch Every Day
Non-negotiable · Joy only · No questioning · Nervous system regulation
Why This Exists and Why It Cannot Be Skipped
After lunch, students are dysregulated. They have been in the cafeteria, on the playground, in transition. The read aloud is not an instructional activity — it is a regulation tool. It signals: the hard part of the day is over, we are together, we are safe, someone is taking care of us. No questions. No comprehension check. No discussion required. Just read.
SAY: "Sit on your spots. Get comfortable. I am going to read to you. Just listen."
Read it. That is all.
🧰 TIP: This read aloud is also for the teacher. It is 10 minutes in the middle of the day where nothing is required of anyone except listening. Protect it.

Shutdown Sequence — Same Every Day
Students who know what comes next do not ask what comes next
  1. Call 4 students at a time for snack — sit, eat, do not rush
  2. Pack up: open zipper → folder in → close zipper → backpack on → push chair in
  3. Line up at door — hot lava line
  4. Walk to specials — 2:25
  5. Return from specials — 3:20
  6. SEL or Science/SS — 10 minutes
  7. Dismiss by ticket color — 3:30 — use the list — never guess
🚨 Dismissal — Never Guess
Any student whose dismissal is unclear — flag it before 3:25. Do not guess. Do not send a student out the wrong door. Call the office. Hold the student. This is the one non-negotiable that overrides everything else in the building.

Center Routines — Teach Before You Use Them
Every center has a procedure · Teach it before students use it · Never assume
The "When I'm Done" Anchor Chart
Every student who finishes a center activity goes to the "When I'm Done" chart. Options listed: read a book from the library bin, draw in your journal, practice snap words with flashcards, work on your writing folder. This chart is the answer to "I'm done what do I do" — forever. Point to it. Do not answer the question verbally.
How to Use the Library Corner
Take one book. Find your reading spot. Hold the book right side up. Turn pages from right to left. When done — return the book to the bin spine up. These are explicitly taught. Five year olds do not infer how to handle books. Teach it.
SuccessMaker Center
Log in using your individual card. Put headphones on before you start. Work independently. When the session is over — log out, put headphones away, go to "When I'm Done." The teacher is with another group. Do not interrupt unless there is an emergency.

Performance Skills — Embedded in Brain Breaks All Year
Progressive · No labels · Students learn these as games · They are building real skills
September — Body Control
Freeze like a statue. Hold it for 5 seconds. Freeze in a different shape. Students learn to control their bodies on command. This is the foundation of everything else.
October — Mirroring
Face a partner. One person leads. The other mirrors every movement exactly — slow and exaggerated. Switch. Students build presence, listening, and physical awareness.
November — Imaginary Objects
Pretend you are holding something very heavy. Something tiny. Something burning hot. Show us with your whole body — not just your hands. Students build imagination and physical specificity.
December — Sense Memory
What does cinnamon smell like? Show us on your face. What does ice feel like? Show us with your whole body. Students connect physical sensation to expression.
January — Given Circumstances
You just found out school is cancelled. React. You just found a puppy outside. React. Simple emotional circumstances with a clear physical response.
February — Listening (Meisner)
Say one word back and forth with a partner. Copy the energy exactly — not the word, the energy. Students build the skill of actually listening and responding to what they hear.
March — Objectives
You REALLY want something. You cannot say it out loud. Show us using only your body. Simple wants drive physical action.
April — Character Physicalization
Be a character who is VERY tired. VERY excited. VERY clumsy. Make the whole body show it — not just the face.
May — Voice Work
Put your hand on your belly. Breathe so your hand moves. Now make a sound from that same place — not from your throat. Hum. Let the vibration move. Students learn to use their breath and resonance.
June — Full Performance
Put it all together. Short scenes. Improv games. Students perform for each other. Everyone gets a standing ovation.
