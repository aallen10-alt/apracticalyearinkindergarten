Arrival — Thursday · Math Page at Seats
9:20 · Math morning work face-down at every seat · Sheep timer already counting down · Students flip it over and begin
9:20–9:40
🚪
Stand at the classroom door. Greet every single student by name as they walk in.
Make eye contact. Use their name. That's it. Students walk in, unpack, find their seat, flip over their math morning work page, and begin. The sheep timer is already running.
SAY to each student as they walk in: "Good morning [name]! Math page is at your seat — flip it over and get started."
⭐ THURSDAY ROUTINE: Math morning work is at seats face-down. Students flip it over when they sit. This is not explained every day — by Week 7 they know. If a student asks what to do, point to the page. Do not explain verbally.
🧰 TOOLBOX: Students who arrive late — point to their seat, mouth "math page," keep greeting at the door. Do not stop your greeting flow for late arrivals. The other adult handles late students.
👥
Another adult circulates — helps students who are stuck on the math page.
Do not give answers. Ask: "What number is that? Can you count these?" Students work independently. The adult redirects off-task students quietly.

Morning Meeting + Phonics · Module 2 Lesson 4 · Consonant n
9:40 · Thursday = Slides + Magnetic Letterboards · Consonant n · Snap Word: to
~50 min
RF.K.2aRF.K.2bRF.K.3RF.K.3bSL.K.1
Objective
Students will identify the /n/ sound at the beginning of words, blend and segment CVC words containing n, and build words using magnetic letter tiles on their letterboards.
🐑
Sheep timer goes off → students are already at bumblebee spots → begin greeting immediately.
By Week 7 students know this. If some are still at their seats when the timer goes off — do not repeat the expectation verbally. Walk to your bumblebee spot, sit down, and begin the greeting without them. They will join. Stopping the group to wait for stragglers teaches stragglers that the group waits.
⭐ THURSDAY GREETING: Students find a partner sitting next to them on the rug. They do a high five and say "Good morning [name], I'm glad you're here." Model it once first: "Watch me — I turn to [student], I look at their eyes, high five, and I say: Good morning [name], I'm glad you're here. Now you try with the person next to you. Go."
🎵
Activity 1 on bumblebee spots — syllable segmentation warm-up — 2 minutes.
SAY: "We are going to clap the syllables in some words. Listen: NEC-TAR-INE. Clap with me — NEC [clap] TAR [clap] INE [clap]. Three syllables. Let's try another one: NUT. NUT [clap]. One syllable. Your turn — NOODLE."
Students clap syllables together. Do 4-5 words, all starting with N so students are primed for the letter sound.
🎵
Activity 2 on bumblebee spots — initial sound isolation — 2 minutes.
SAY: "Now I want you to listen for the very first sound in these words. Just the first sound — not the whole word. NEST. What is the very first sound in NEST? [pause for responses] /n/. Yes. The first sound is /n/. Let's try: NUT. What's the first sound? [pause] /n/. NET. First sound? [pause] /n/. CAT. First sound? [pause] /k/ — that one's different, good listening."
Do 6-8 words — mostly N words, 2 distractors. Students call out the first sound.
📋
Sing to smartboard spots → read morning message → letter sound cards.
🎵 SING: "Smartboard spots, smartboard spots, find your smartboard spot!" Students move to their smartboard spots. Do not walk them there — the song is the signal.
Read the morning message together. Point to each word as you read. Students echo.
MORNING MESSAGE (Thursday — reusable all month): "Today is Thursday. We will use our letterboards today. Our snap word is [to]. Look at our schedule for today."
Point to the daily schedule posted on the board. Name each block out loud: "First we do phonics. Then math. Then shared reading. Then lunch. After lunch I read to you. Then guided reading. Then specials. Then we go home." Students who know what comes next do not ask what comes next.

Letter sound cards — hold up each card, students call out the sound — every single card in the deck — 3 minutes — no exceptions, no skipping, no "we'll do it tomorrow."
⭐ LETTER SOUND CARDS — ALWAYS: Even on the craziest day. Even on Halloween. Even when you are behind. Three minutes. Every card. This is the non-negotiable that builds automaticity. Skip it once and you teach students it can be skipped.

Phonics Lesson — Consonant n — Slides + Magnetic Letterboards
Pull up the slide for the letter N. Hold up the letter N card from the letter sound deck.
SAY: "This is the letter N. It makes the /n/ sound. Put your tongue behind your top teeth and hum — /n/. Feel that? That is where the N sound lives. Say it with me — /n/. /n/. /n/."

Now show the letterboard.
SAY: "Today we are using our letterboards. Look down at your letterboard — you should have a tray of letters in front of you. Do NOT touch them yet. Eyes on me."
Wait until every student has stopped touching the letters and is looking at you.
SAY: "Find the letter N in your tray. Big N — capital N. Hold it up so I can see it." [scan the room] "Good. Now find the little n — lowercase n. Hold that one up." [scan] "Perfect. Put both of them at the top of your letterboard — they live there. Now listen."

Build CVC words together — step by step:
SAY: "We are going to build the word NAP. N-A-P. Three sounds. What's the first sound in NAP? /n/ — find your lowercase n and put it on your board. What's the middle sound? /ă/ — find your a. What's the last sound? /p/ — find your p. Now you have N-A-P. Read it with me — NAP."
Do this for 4 words: NAP, NET, NIP, NOT. For each one: say the word → tap the sounds → students find each letter → build it → read it together.
🧰 TOOLBOX: Students who can't find the letter — do not find it for them. Say: "The letter N looks like two lines with a line connecting them at the top. Keep looking." Give them 10 seconds. Then if they're still stuck, point to it without touching their tray.
🧰 TOOLBOX — LETTERBOARD ROUTINE: When you're done with a word — "Clear your board. Slide all the letters back into your tray." Do not move to the next word until every board is clear. This takes 10 seconds and prevents chaos.
⚡
Snap Word — "to" — introduce at smartboard spots.
SAY: "Time for our snap word. Ready?" [snap fingers] "The word is TO. T-O. Spell it with me — T, O. Find the letter T in your tray. Find the letter O. Build the word TO on your board. Show me." [scan] "Now clear your boards and put your trays to the side. We're done with letterboards."
Add TO to the snap word wall. Count how many snap words are on the wall now.
SAY: "We have [number] snap words on our wall. Every time you see one of these words in a book — you already know it. You know it in a snap."
🧠
Brain break — 2 minutes — imaginary objects — before math.
SAY: "Stand up. You are holding something very, very heavy. Show me with your whole body — not just your hands. Your whole body. The thing is SO heavy. Now — put it down. Now pick up something tiny — so tiny it could fit on your fingertip. Show me how carefully you hold it. Now — put it in your pocket. Sit back down."
This is 90 seconds. It resets the nervous system before math. Students don't know they're developing performance skills — they think it's a fun break.

Math · Unit 3 Lesson 1 · Flat Shapes — Identify Circles, Squares, Triangles, Rectangles
Hook → bumblebee spots · Mini lesson → smartboard spots · Seat work · ⭐ Introduce Math Shelf center today · Groups
~75 min
K.G.A.1K.G.A.2K.G.B.4MP.6MP.7
Objective
Students will identify, name, and describe flat shapes — circle, square, triangle, rectangle — by their attributes. Students will find flat shapes in the real world around the classroom.
🔵
Hook on bumblebee spots — 3 minutes — curiosity only — do not teach yet.
Hold up a book. Don't say anything. Hold it up and look at it like you're noticing something for the first time.
SAY: "I was looking at this book this morning and I noticed something. The cover — what shape is it? Don't answer yet. Look around the room. How many things can you find that are that same shape before I count to ten? Look — don't touch. Ready? 1... 2... 3..." [count slowly] "10. Come to your smartboard spots and tell me what you found."
🎵 SING: "Smartboard spots, smartboard spots, find your smartboard spot!"
📐
Mini lesson on smartboard spots — 8 minutes — four shapes — manipulatives only.
Hold up a large foam or cardboard circle.
SAY: "This is a circle. A circle is round — it has no straight sides and no corners. Run your finger around the edge — round the whole way. No bumps. No corners. That's what makes it a circle." [hold it up] "Say it with me — circle."
Hold up a square.
SAY: "This is a square. A square has four sides — let's count them: 1, 2, 3, 4. And four corners — corners are the pointy parts where the sides meet: 1, 2, 3, 4. Here's the special thing about a square — all four sides are the same length. They match. Say it — square."
Hold up a triangle.
SAY: "This is a triangle. How many sides? Count with me: 1, 2, 3. Three sides. How many corners? 1, 2, 3. Three corners. TRI means three — like tricycle has three wheels. TRI-angle. Say it — triangle."
Hold up a rectangle.
SAY: "This is a rectangle. It also has four sides and four corners — just like a square. But look — these two sides are long, and these two sides are short. A square's sides are all the same. A rectangle's sides are not all the same. That's the difference. Say it — rectangle."

Now do a quick check: hold up each shape, students call out the name. Then mix them up and do it faster.
🧰 TOOLBOX: Students will call a rectangle a square. This is developmentally normal. Do not correct harshly — redirect: "It has four sides and four corners like a square — but look at this side and this side. Are they the same length? No — so what does that make it? A rectangle."

Brain break — 2 minutes — then seat work with visible timer.
Quick GoNoodle or freeze dance — 90 seconds — then students go to seats.
SAY: "I am setting the timer for 8 minutes. Your job is to finish the shape page before the timer goes off. When you are done — flip it face down and put your hands flat on the table. That tells me you're done without saying a word. Timer starts — now."
Set the visible timer. Circulate for 3 minutes checking in. Then begin setting up groups.
⭐ TIMER EXPECTATION: Timer goes off. Student is not done. Quiet conversation: "The timer went off and your page isn't finished. What happened?" Listen. If the student was off-task — this is when the accountability slip conversation happens. Not publicly. Quietly, at their seat, while other students transition to centers.
⭐
⭐ Introduce the Math Shelf — Do this before groups start — everyone watching.
Walk to the math shelf. Students are still at their seats after seat work. Do this as a whole group demo before anyone moves.
⭐ CENTER INTRODUCTION — MATH SHELF
SAY: "I want to show you a new center before we start groups today. This is the math shelf. [point to it] When it is your turn at the math shelf, here is exactly what you do. Step one — walk to the shelf. Don't run. Step two — find the bin that says SHAPES on it — it has a picture of a shape on the front. Step three — take the bin to your spot on the rug. Do not open it yet. Step four — open the bin and take out what's inside. [hold up the sorting cards and shape cut-outs] Today on the math shelf you are sorting shapes. You will put each shape on the card that matches it. Step five — when the timer goes off, you put everything back in the bin, close the lid, and put the bin back on the shelf exactly where you found it. Then you go to the When I'm Done chart and pick an activity. Let me show you what it looks like when someone does it wrong." [demo the wrong way — take out pieces, leave them scattered, don't put bin back] "What did I do wrong?" [take responses] "Now let me show you the right way." [demo correctly] "Any questions about the math shelf? This is the only time I will explain it. After today — you already know."
🎯
Small group rotations — 2 rotations × 20 min — 4 groups of 5-6 students.
Teacher table — pull Group 1:
Students come to the teacher table with their seat work page. Work through the shape page together using physical foam shapes. For each shape — touch it, name it, count the sides, count the corners. Then do the sorting activity: mix up 8-10 shapes, students sort them by name.
AT THE TEACHER TABLE — SAY: "Pick up the circle. How do you know it's a circle? Tell me — not just the name, tell me why. [student explains] Right — no straight sides, no corners, round all the way around. Now find me a square."
If a student finishes early — "Can you build a picture using only shapes? A house, a robot, whatever you want — only flat shapes."

Adult 2 table — same activity — Group 2.

Math shelf — Group 3 — shape sorting independently.
Students already know the routine from the intro. Circulate briefly to make sure they're doing it correctly.

SuccessMaker — Group 4 — headphones on — independent.
🧰 TOOLBOX — ASKING FOR HELP AT CENTERS: Students at independent centers who need help must say: "I don't understand how to ____." Students who say "I'm done" or "I don't know what to do" — point to the When I'm Done chart. Do not answer "I don't know what to do" verbally.
⭐ AFTER EVERY MATH TEST: The game that students played at the teacher table becomes the new math shelf game. This is how students learn to run centers independently — they were already taught the game by the teacher.

Shared Reading · Module 2 Week 1 · Thursday
ABC I Like Me! Big Book · Big Book + Closer Look + myBook response · Thursday format
~38 min
RL.K.1RL.K.6RL.K.7RF.K.1aSL.K.2
Essential Question — Module 2
What makes each of us special? Students revisit the Big Book with a closer look at how illustrations and text work together to tell us about a character.
📚
Read ABC I Like Me! Big Book — closer look — focus on one page at a time.
Hold the Big Book so every student can see. Thursday is not a first read — students have seen this book or its companion this week. Go slower. Look closer.
SAY: "We already heard this book once this week. Today we are going to be detectives. I am going to stop on some pages and we are going to look really carefully at what the pictures are telling us — things the words don't even say. Ready? Detectives, look at this page."
Stop on 3-4 pages. For each one — cover the words with your hand and ask: "What is happening in this picture? What is the character feeling? How do you know?" Then uncover the words: "Did the words tell us the same thing, or something different?"
🧰 TOOLBOX: Thursday shared reading is about depth, not breadth. You do not need to read every page. Pick 3-4 pages and really dig into them. This teaches comprehension skills more effectively than racing through the whole book.
📝
myBook response — students draw one thing they like about themselves.
SAY: "Open your myBook. Find the page about what makes you special. Today I want you to draw — or write if you can — one thing you like about yourself. It can be something you're good at, something about how you look, or something about how you treat other people. Put your name on the line at the top first. Then draw. You have 10 minutes."
Circulate. Sit next to 2-3 students and have a quiet conversation: "Tell me about your picture." Write their words at the bottom if they want.

Teacher's Read Aloud — After Lunch · Every Day · Non-Negotiable
1:30 · Bathroom train first → bumblebee spots → read · Joy only · No questions ever
~10 min
🚻
Bathroom train the moment students return — before anything else.
⭐ BATHROOM TRAIN ROUTINE: Students line up. First person goes in, does what they need to do, comes out, taps the next person. No talking in the hallway. No saving spots. The train moves. Everyone goes before the read aloud — no interruptions during the book.
📚
Bumblebee spots. Get comfortable. Read aloud. No questions. No discussion.
SAY: "Sit on your spots. You can lean against someone if you want. I am going to read to you. All you have to do is listen."
Read. That's it. When the book is over — don't ask "what was your favorite part." Don't ask anything. Let the book settle. Take a breath. Move to the next thing.

October read aloud suggestions: Creepy Carrots → Room on the Broom → Go Away Big Green Monster → Skippyjon Jones → Too Much Glue →

Guided Reading / Centers / Flex Block
1:40–2:05 · Pull whoever needs it most · Centers run simultaneously
~25 min
📚
Pull the group that needs the most support today — based on what you saw this morning.
If consonant n was hard — pull those students. If snap word "to" didn't stick — pull those students. Even 8 minutes of targeted instruction at this table moves a student further than 25 minutes of whole-group review.
AT THE GUIDED READING TABLE: "We're going to read this book together. When we get to a word you don't know — don't skip it. Don't guess randomly. Look at the first letter and think: what sound does that letter make? Then look at the picture. Then try the word. Let's start."
🧰 TOOLBOX: You do not need to pull every group every day. Pull who needs you. The other students are at independent centers — they are fine. Your time at the table is more valuable than your time circulating.

Social Studies · Community Helpers · Lesson 3

~10 min
SS.K.C.1SS.K.CC.1
🏛️
Community Helpers who keep us safe — police officers, firefighters, crossing guards.
Show a photo of each. Ask: "What does this person do? How do they help our community?"
SAY: "We have talked about community helpers who help us when we are sick — doctors and nurses. Today we are looking at community helpers who keep us safe. [show photo of police officer] This is a police officer. What is one thing a police officer does to help us? Turn and tell your partner." [30 seconds] "Now [show firefighter] this is a firefighter. Think about it."
Quick draw — students draw one community helper on a half sheet. Hold up and share 3-4. Keep it moving — 10 minutes total.
